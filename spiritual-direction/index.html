Spiritual Direction

We are in the early stages of building a network of spiritual directors whose practice is explicitly liberationist, trauma-informed, and radically inclusive. These directors will not share a single method or spiritual style, but they will share core commitments: to consent, to respect for boundaries, to restorative justice, and to an understanding of spiritual growth that does not require fear, shame, or erasure.

Spiritual direction is a practice of attentive presence, a shared listening for what is alive, stirring, or asking to be named in a person’s life. It makes space for prayer, reflection, silence, and honest exploration, trusting that God meets people as they are. At its heart, spiritual direction is about listening for the Spirit together and joining in a posture of openness to Christ.

It is not advice, correction, diagnosis, or spiritual performance. It is not about fixing faith, managing behavior, or submitting to an expert or authority figure. Within the Liberating Scripture Collective, spiritual direction explicitly resists shame, coercion, and the use of religious language as a means of control.

Instead, spiritual direction names a form of accompaniment, walking alongside another person with care, curiosity, and respect. It honors agency, attends to the body and lived experience, and trusts that the Spirit’s work unfolds over time through relationship, honesty, and consent.

Within the Liberating Scripture Collective, spiritual direction is held alongside Bible translation, teaching, and public conversation, but it is not reducible to any of them. It is a relational practice that takes seriously the ways faith, scripture, and theology have shaped people’s lives, sometimes as sources of healing and meaning, and sometimes as sources of trauma and harm. This work makes room for both realities in all the messy and uncomfortable experiences they bring.

This future directory is being shaped for people who may be asking questions like these:

  • How do I remain open to God after religious harm or exhaustion?
  • How do I discern what is life-giving without surrendering my agency?
  • How do I pray or listen for the Spirit without reenacting old patterns of pressure or performance?
  • How do I integrate faith, justice, healing, and honesty in a way that honors my body and story?

As this work develops, this space will host a curated list of spiritual directors whose practice aligns with the commitments of the Liberation and Inclusion Translation and the broader work of the Collective. Participation will not be based on credentials alone, but on demonstrated ethical clarity, theological transparency, and respect for the dignity and autonomy of those they accompany.

If you are a spiritual director whose work is rooted in liberation, inclusion, and trauma-aware care, you are welcome to reach out through the contact page here.

If you are seeking accompaniment, this space will evolve as the work takes shape. For now, we invite you to stay tuned.

Just as spiritual direction is an experience of careful and attentive relationship, so too will this gathering of spiritual directors be done with care and attention to relationships.
